Piezometric surface is not (generally) the water table

ENCN342 — Hydraulics and Applied Hydrology
University of Canterbury

A very common picture is wrong: that groundwater sits in an underground lake with a surface you could touch, and that the line drawn on a cross-section is that surface.

What is actually true: that picture works for the water table of an unconfined aquifer. For a confined aquifer the piezometric surface is a hypothetical level — the height water would rise to in a tube — and there is nothing physically there. It can sit above the ground surface. The two panels below use the same instrument (paired piezometers) in almost the same ground so you can see the difference directly.

Where this fits: use after Module 1 (Groundwater Systems), before Tutorial 1 Q2 and before anything involving drawdown. Every conceptual-sketch exam question and every Theis calculation assumes you understand this.
