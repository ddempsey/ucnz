Storage, recharge and capture

ENCN342 — Hydraulics and Applied Hydrology
University of Canterbury

Most people start with the same picture. An aquifer holds a certain amount of water, rain tops it up each year, and if you pump less than the recharge you are taking a fair share.

The water balance does not really bear that out. Once the system settles, almost none of the water in your well is coming out of storage. It comes from capture: recharge you have increased, and discharges you have taken away. Usually it is mostly the discharges, which means streams, springs and wetlands. So if you pump at something like the average recharge rate, you are probably taking most of what those used to get — though how much depends on how much extra recharge the pumping manages to induce.

This misconception has a name in the profession, the water-budget myth, and hydrogeologists have been correcting it since 1940. With limited success:

“Perhaps the most common misconception in groundwater hydrology is that a water budget of an area determines the magnitude of possible groundwater development. Several well-known hydrologists have addressed this misconception and attempted to dispel it. Somehow, though, it persists and continues to color decisions by the water-management community.” — Bredehoeft, Papadopulos & Cooper (1982)
